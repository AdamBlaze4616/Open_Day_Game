Research & writing

Turning university knowledge into community impact.

Co-authored research paper
Learn, Ask, Connect: Turning University Cyber and AI Knowledge into Community Impact
Mary George • Adam Mardiryan • David Bonnett • Ricardo Krestevski • Associate Professor Leanne Ngo

The paper presents Melbourne Companions as a community-project case exploring how students, academics and community partners can work together to deliver cyber and AI safety support that can be adapted for different communities while also building students’ practical skills.

~50older community members received direct support in the first City of Kingston program
100+people were involved across the wider initiative
A$6,000additional 2026 funding following Ford C3 recognition at the Australian Enactus National Competition

Learn

Practical cyber and AI safety education covering scams, phishing, safe online practices, AI-generated content and deepfakes, with material reviewed by cybersecurity academics.

Ask

Participants can ask about risks that matter to them. A digital drop-in session extended the formal workshop and created space for one-to-one questions about suspicious content, deepfakes and online trust.

Connect

Community members connect directly with trained university students who adjust their pace and examples to different levels of digital experience, with support provided in preferred languages where possible, including Mandarin.

Adapt

The model is designed to change with each community. Future delivery can adapt language, examples, priority risks, volunteer training, room layout and resources to local needs.

Responsible research framing: the project team used documents, attendance records, workshop materials, plans and delivery notes. Informal comments were used to improve future sessions, not as a formal research evaluation, so the paper does not claim measured changes in participant knowledge, confidence or behaviour.

The work also documents lessons from real delivery: the importance of personalised support, cultural awareness and privacy boundaries; requests for translated materials; the need for better visibility during demonstrations; and the complementary roles of students and academics in keeping information practical, accurate and safe. The 2026 expansion is aimed at regional, rural and underserved communities, including refugee communities and women-focused groups. The paper links the project to lifelong learning, partnerships, student employability and stronger community-based support.

Community impact

Security should help people.

The part of cybersecurity I care about most is making knowledge useful to people who may not have a security team, specialist training or someone they trust to ask.

♡

Melbourne Companions — Cyber & AI Safety

Through Enactus La Trobe, LTUCS and WiCyS, I help deliver cyber and AI safety education for older and underserved community members. Sessions cover phishing, impersonation, phone and online scams, deepfakes, AI-generated content, passwords, privacy and safe online practices. The emphasis is on accessible language and genuine conversation rather than simply telling people to “be careful.”

🛡

Scambaiting & Social Engineering Defence

I independently study active scam campaigns and social-engineering tactics, including how scammers establish trust, create urgency, impersonate organisations and pressure victims. I use ethical scambaiting and analysis to better understand attacker behaviour and translate those lessons into practical scam-prevention advice, digital-footprint awareness and cyber hygiene education.

🤝

LTUCS × Enactus × WiCyS Collaboration

I work across student societies and social-impact groups rather than treating cybersecurity as an isolated discipline.
