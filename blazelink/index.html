Research & writing

Co-writing research from real community work.

I am a co-writer of the Melbourne Companions research paper documenting how university cyber and AI knowledge can be translated into practical, adaptable community support.

Co-authored research paper
Learn, Ask, Connect: Turning University Cyber and AI Knowledge into Community Impact
Mary George • Adam Mardiryan • David Bonnett • Ricardo Krestevski • Associate Professor Leanne Ngo

The paper examines Melbourne Companions as a student-led cyber and AI safety project delivered through Enactus La Trobe in collaboration with cybersecurity student societies, academics, local government and community organisations. It asks how students, academics and community partners can provide cyber and AI safety support that adapts to different communities while also developing students' professional and communication skills.

~50older community members received direct support during the first City of Kingston program
100+people were involved across the broader initiative
A$6,000additional funding followed 2026 Ford C3 recognition at the Australian Enactus National Competition

Learn

Participants learn practical cyber and AI safety concepts including scams, phishing, safer online behaviour, AI-generated content and deepfakes. Cybersecurity academics review content to help keep guidance accurate, appropriate and safe.

Ask

The model gives community members room to ask about risks they actually encounter. During the first delivery, participants raised questions about deepfakes, AI-generated videos and how to tell whether suspicious content was genuine.

Connect

Participants connect directly with trained university students during interactive sessions and digital drop-in support. Students adjust pace and examples to different levels of digital experience, with preferred-language support provided where possible, including Mandarin.

Adapt

The model is designed to change for each community rather than force the same workshop everywhere. Language, examples, volunteer training, room design and priority risks can be adjusted with councils, libraries, neighbourhood houses and community partners.

Responsible research practice: project documents, attendance records, workshop material, plans and delivery notes were used to describe the case. Informal comments were used to improve later delivery rather than presented as formal impact data, so the paper does not claim measured changes in participant knowledge, confidence or behaviour.

The project identified practical lessons around one-to-one support, cultural awareness, privacy boundaries, translated resources, visibility of live demonstrations and the different but connected roles of students and academics. The model is now being adapted for regional, rural and underserved settings, including refugee communities and women-focused groups, while further funding supports expansion.

Community impact

Cybersecurity should help people feel safer, not more confused.

The human side of security is one of my strongest interests. Strong technical controls matter, but attackers often target trust, fear, urgency and uncertainty. That is why scam awareness, social-engineering education and accessible cyber support are a major part of my work.

♡

Melbourne Companions — Cyber & AI Safety

Through Enactus La Trobe, LTUCS and WiCyS, I help deliver interactive cyber and AI safety education to older and underserved community members. Topics include phishing, impersonation, phone scams, online fraud, fake websites, passwords, privacy, AI-generated images and video, deepfakes and practical ways to verify suspicious content. The emphasis is plain language, conversation and one-to-one support rather than a one-way technical lecture.

🛡

Scambaiting & Social Engineering Defence

I independently research scam campaigns and social-engineering tactics to better understand how attackers build credibility, create urgency, impersonate trusted organisations and manipulate victims. Ethical scambaiting and phishing analysis have helped me recognise recurring behavioural patterns and turn those lessons into safer, practical education around digital footprints, phishing recognition and cyber hygiene.
